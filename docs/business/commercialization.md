# Commercialization hypotheses

A globe can attract visitors, but it does not establish demand for paid analysis. The product's value must come from a repeatable decision a customer would pay to improve.

Start with one narrow mining or environmental monitoring workflow: monthly site-change screening plus a reviewable evidence report. Sentinel-2 can support broad surface/vegetation/water screening; it cannot establish construction progress at individual-house detail or prove excavation causality. Confirm changes with higher-resolution imagery or field evidence where the decision requires it.

Real estate: large development footprints and historical context, not property valuation or legal ownership. Mining: vegetation/disturbance screening, not certified excavation volumes or illegal-mining conclusions. Government: procurement, tender conditions, data licensing and validation can dominate sales timelines. Defence: private deployment and authorized historical analysis only; no classified-data handling or certification claims.

Before pricing, interview five buyers in one segment and request their current workflow, decision frequency, cost of false alarms, required resolution and budget. Seek two paid pilot commitments with written acceptance criteria. Proposed pilot pricing is a hypothesis, not validated revenue. Measure analyst hours, image/compute cost and false-positive review effort per report before promising margins.

Cost drivers: raster reads and egress, higher-resolution imagery licenses, persistent jobs, queue/database/storage, monitoring, support and expert review. Free exploration does not imply unlimited free processing. No paid plans or billing were activated in this build. Review current provider limits and commercial terms before selling a plan; no fixed monthly cost or unlimited-free promise is made.
